Roots and Wings

Growing secure, confident, connected families.

Roots and Wings is Tulip’s family formation programme. Each edition takes one relationship in the family and works it from both sides at once: a set of sessions for each person, and a bridge exercise where the two of you meet and read your answers aloud.

Roots means secure attachment and belonging. Wings means healthy autonomy, identity and responsibility. The programme exists because no family should have to trade one for the other.

The method

Two people. Two journeys. One room.

Every edition of Roots and Wings is built the same way. Two people in the same family do parallel work on the same theme at the same time, privately at first, and then together. Nobody is asked to sit and be discussed.

Track one

Private reflection

Each person works their own set of sessions alone. An insight to read, questions to answer honestly, an activity, and a journal prompt. There are no right answers, only true ones.

Track two

The same theme, the other chair

The second person works the mirror of that session, written for their position in the family. Same subject, different seat, so both arrive at the meeting having actually prepared.

The bridge

Then the two of you meet. Complete your side privately first. Take turns reading aloud. No defending, no explaining, no correcting while the other person reads. Sit with it for one quiet minute, then end with one line of appreciation to each other.

Adolescents and their parents

For young people aged 11 to 17 and the parent or carer walking beside them. Ten sessions for the adolescent, ten mirrored sessions for the parent, and six bridge conversations along the way. Adolescence is not detachment. It is the reorganisation of attachment, and this edition is built to help a family survive that reorganisation with the relationship intact.

Single parents

Raising alone, without carrying it alone.

A single parent holds two chairs at once and is rarely offered either. This edition pairs the parent’s own sessions with a track for the child old enough to notice what is being carried, and brings them together at the bridge. It works the practical questions and the quiet ones with the same seriousness.

  • The load, named honestly
  • The absent parent who is still in the room
  • Boundaries without a second adult
  • Guilt, and what it is actually asking for
  • When a child becomes a confidant
  • Building a wider circle of safe adults
  • Money, time and the things that will not stretch
  • What a child actually needs, which is less than you fear

Blended families

Two histories, one household.

A blended family is asked to behave like a family that has had years of practice, immediately. This edition slows that expectation down. It pairs the step parent and the child, and separately the two adults, so that loyalty, authority and pace can be spoken about rather than fought about.

  • Loyalty binds, and the child caught between
  • What authority a step parent actually has
  • The pace of joining, which cannot be rushed
  • The other household, and how to speak of it
  • New siblings, old territory
  • House rules that two histories can both accept
  • Grief living inside a new beginning
  • The couple, protected as the load bearing wall
