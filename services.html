What we do, and who it is for.

Tulip works with families, couples, individuals, young people and the institutions that hold them. Therapy and accredited mediation sit side by side, so the same practice can help a family understand one another and reach a workable agreement.

01

Family therapy

The whole system in the room, whether something has broken or you would like to keep it from breaking.

Most families arrive describing one person as the problem. Family therapy works instead with the pattern between people, because the pattern is usually what keeps the difficulty in place. Sessions are structured but not scripted, and everyone present is given a turn that nobody is allowed to interrupt.

It is also worth saying plainly that this is not only a repair service. Families are welcome here before anything has gone wrong, at the point where a child is about to enter secondary school, where a parent is about to travel, where a new person is about to join the household. Coming early is the cheapest and most effective work a family can do.

Come here if

  • Conversations end in the same argument
  • One child is carrying the family’s distress
  • A loss, illness or separation has shifted everything
  • You have stopped being able to hear each other
  • Nothing is wrong, and you would like to keep it that way

What to expect

  • An initial session to map the family and its history
  • Sessions of about ninety minutes
  • Work with whoever attends, no full attendance required
  • A review at six sessions to decide whether to continue

02

Adolescent therapy

For young people aged 11 to 17, with parents held alongside rather than shut out.

Adolescence asks a family to reorganise, not to separate. A young person needs somewhere to think that is genuinely theirs, and a parent needs to know the work is going somewhere. Tulip holds both: the young person has confidential space, and parents are given their own parallel guidance so nobody is left outside the process.

Come here if

  • Withdrawal, anger or silence has become the norm
  • School, friendships or mood have changed sharply
  • Behaviour looks like defiance and may be distress
  • You are losing a child who still lives at home

What to expect

  • The young person is treated as the client, not the case
  • Clear, age appropriate limits on confidentiality
  • Parent sessions run in parallel
  • Often paired with the Roots and Wings workbook

03

Children’s therapy

Age appropriate work for younger children, most often held inside the family session.

Young children rarely need to be taken away and fixed. They need the adults around them to become more predictable, and they need a way of showing what they cannot yet explain. Work with children therefore usually happens within family sessions, using play, drawing and story rather than interview, with individual time offered where it genuinely helps.

Come here if

  • Sleep, appetite or behaviour has changed
  • A separation, bereavement or move has unsettled the home
  • School has raised a concern
  • A child is anxious, clingy or unusually angry

What to expect

  • Held to a certified child safeguarding standard
  • Play, drawing and story rather than questioning
  • Parents coached in what to do between sessions
  • Referral onward where a specialist assessment is needed

04

Individual therapy

For one person doing their own work, including single adults preparing themselves before marriage rather than after.

A great deal of what goes wrong in a marriage arrived long before the marriage did. Patricia sees single adults who want to understand their own patterns, their family of origin and their expectations while there is still room to choose, which is the least glamorous and most useful preparation available to anyone.

Others come alone for reasons that have nothing to do with marriage: a decision, a loss, anxiety, a difficult parent, or simply the sense of repeating something without knowing why.

Come here if

  • You are single and want to know yourself before you marry
  • The same pattern keeps repeating in your relationships
  • You are carrying something from your own childhood
  • You are facing a decision, a loss or a change

What to expect

  • One to one sessions, in person or online
  • Premarital work alone, and later with a partner if you wish
  • Attention to your family of origin, not only the present
  • Referral onward where specialist care is the right answer

05

Couples therapy

For the partnership underneath the parenting, which usually needs attention first.

Many families present a child’s difficulty and discover a couple who have not spoken to each other, rather than about logistics, in a long time. Couples work here is practical. It slows the argument down far enough to see the cycle, and it teaches each partner to say the thing underneath the complaint.

Come here if

  • The same argument repeats with different content
  • You are managing a household rather than a marriage
  • Trust has been damaged and you want to try
  • You are deciding whether to stay or to separate well

What to expect

  • Both partners seen together, with occasional individual sessions
  • Neutrality held firmly, no side taken
  • Focus on the cycle rather than the last incident
  • Separation supported honestly where that is the outcome

06

Parent consultation

For parents who want to change the pattern without trying to change the child.

Sometimes the young person will not come, and sometimes they should not have to. Parent consultation works with the adults alone, because a change in how a parent responds will move a family system whether or not the child ever attends a session. Much of it is reflective, and some of it is inherited: a good deal of parenting begins long before the child.

Come here if

  • Your teenager refuses to attend therapy
  • You and your co parent respond very differently
  • You recognise your own parents in your reactions
  • You want to hold a boundary without losing the relationship

What to expect

  • Sessions for one parent or both
  • Practical work on what to do this week
  • Attention to your own attachment history
  • Often paired with the Roots and Wings parent track

07

Mediation

Interest based, non adversarial dispute resolution, accredited with the Kenya Judiciary.

Mediation is for the moment when a family needs a decision, not only an understanding. As an accredited Mediation Practitioner, Patricia helps parties identify a solution together rather than have one imposed. The process is collaborative and confidential, and it is built so that all voices are heard and nobody has to lose for the family to move.

Come here if

  • Separation or divorce arrangements need settling
  • Parenting plans and care time are disputed
  • Succession, property or family business is contested
  • Extended family conflict has reached deadlock

What to expect

  • Accredited with the Kenya Judiciary
  • Neutral throughout, no advice given to either side
  • Interests explored before positions are traded
  • A written agreement drafted from what you decide

08

Organisations and schools

Staff wellbeing, conflict, and response to loss and grief in schools, churches, boards and institutions.

Teachers, chaplains, matrons and managers spend their days absorbing other people’s distress, and are rarely given anywhere to put it down. Tulip runs sessions for staff teams on the relational health of the people who hold everyone else, and works with leadership on the conflict and communication patterns that quietly shape an institution.

Tulip is also called when a community loses someone. The death of a pupil, a colleague or a member of a congregation reaches a long way past the people who knew them well, and it lands on staff who are expected to keep functioning while grieving. That needs handling deliberately, in the days that follow and again some weeks later, rather than being left to settle by itself.

Come here if

  • A death or serious loss has affected the community
  • Staff are carrying pupil or congregant distress
  • A team is strained by unresolved conflict
  • Boarding staff need supervision and a place to reflect
  • Leadership wants a safeguarding aware culture

What to expect

  • Half day and full day formats, on site or online
  • Loss and grief response, at the time and again later
  • Staff wellbeing and burnout sessions
  • Conflict resolution for teams and boards
  • Programmes tailored to the institution, not off the shelf

09

Talks and seminars

Speaking to audiences on marriage, parenting and family life, in person or online.

Patricia speaks regularly to parent bodies, congregations, staff rooms and conferences. Talks are prepared for the specific audience rather than delivered from a standing deck, and they are built to leave people with something they can use at home that evening rather than only something to agree with.

Topics include

  • Raising adolescents without losing the relationship
  • Attachment, and what behaviour is really saying
  • Conflict without damage in the home
  • Marriage, repair, and the work after the rupture

Formats

  • Keynote or seminar, in person or online
  • Parent evenings and school open days
  • Church, conference and staff retreat sessions
  • Workshop series over several weeks
